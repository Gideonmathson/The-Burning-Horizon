Heat is often represented as a quantity attached to air. Lived heat does not arrive that way. It may first appear as reluctance: the distance to the shop looks longer than it did an hour earlier. A metal railing ceases to be a neutral support and becomes something to avoid. The room reorganizes itself around the fan. A patch of shade acquires the character of a destination.

Phenomenological description begins before explanation. It asks what has changed in the field of possible action. The body does not merely register temperature; it discovers a world in which surfaces, intervals, movements and objects have altered their invitations.

## Horizon

In Husserl, an object appears with more implied than is presently given. Every appearance carries a horizon of possible further appearances. Heat complicates this in a useful way because the horizon is not only visual. The shaded side of a road is anticipated bodily before it is reached. A water bottle is apprehended through the future relief it promises. Noon itself can become a horizon of avoidance.

The question is therefore not only: *What does heat feel like?* It is also: *What sort of world does heat make expectable?*

## Body

Merleau-Ponty gives us a body that is not merely located in space but inhabits it. In severe heat that inhabitation becomes unusually conspicuous. The body's ordinary transparency recedes. Walking, waiting, sleeping, eating, touching and attending acquire thermal weight.

The world is still there. But it is there otherwise.
