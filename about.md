<section class="wrap page-head"><h1>About</h1><p><em>The Burning Horizon</em> is a multi-author blog about heat and phenomenology in equal measure.</p></section><article class="article"><p>Heat is encountered here as temperature, atmosphere, duration, exhaustion, radiation, memory, labour, material process and alteration of the sensible world. Phenomenology is not merely a method applied to heat. It is also an object of reading and argument in its own right.</p><p>The blog moves between close readings of Husserl and Merleau-Ponty, descriptions of lived heat, ethnographic fieldnotes, essays on bodies and cities, thermal science, and contemporary heat news. A newspaper report, a philosophical passage, a hot wall after sunset, a worker changing route for shade, and a question about intentionality can all belong here without being made equivalent.</p><p>The wager is that heat changes how a world appears, and that phenomenology gives us unusually exact ways of staying with appearance long enough to describe what has changed.</p></article>
